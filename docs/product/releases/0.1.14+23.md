One theme, and for once it is the thing this project is actually for: a word
should be able to say everything about itself, and nothing anybody made should
be lost on the way in.

A dictionary entry used to be a headword, a gloss and, if somebody had gone out
of their way, two extra forms of a noun. It can now hold the whole word - how it
is said, what it means stated *in Kasem*, how it changes for "the", for many and
for two, how the verb reads now, yesterday and tomorrow, what else the word is
used as, and where it came from. The guided queue, which produces nearly every
entry that arrives, can finally record somebody saying it, and will take a word
we never thought to ask for.

The rest of the release is about not losing things. A post survives the camera
taking the app down with it. A reel stops playing when something covers it.
Explore has a visible way out. And a link posted by an account nobody has
checked is shown, honestly, and dead.

## Full change list

### New - a dictionary entry that holds the whole word

- **Thirteen form slots, one flat map.** `definite plural pluralDefinite counted
  pronoun article` for the thing, `present past future pluralSubject imperative`
  for the action, and `agreeingOne agreeingTwo` for the words whose form is
  chosen by what they attach to. Flat rather than a noun object beside a verb
  object, because a Kasem word is routinely both, and a data model that made the
  contributor choose would record the entry that is genuinely both as whichever
  the dropdown happened to name first.

- **A word used both ways keeps both halves.** `parseLexicalForms(raw, classes)`
  is handed every class the word claims - the declared one *and* everything in
  `alsoUsedAs` - so a noun somebody has told us is also used as a verb publishes
  with its plural and its tenses intact instead of losing whichever half the
  dropdown did not name.

- **Only the answered slots are stored.** Eleven keys of which the median word
  fills none would put ten empty strings on every row and make "not asked"
  indistinguishable from "answered with nothing". `storableForms` writes what
  somebody typed and nothing else; `hasLexicalForms` decides whether the key
  appears at all.

- **The meaning, said in Kasem.** `kasemDefinition` is the only text on the
  record written *in* the language rather than about it, and it is the
  highest-value string this project can collect. The form asks for it as "How
  you would explain it to a child who asked".

- **A transcription, stored without its delimiters.** Half of contributors type
  the slashes and half do not, so `parseIpa` strips them on the way in and
  `ipaDisplay` adds them back on the way out. Stored bare means two people who
  wrote the same pronunciation two ways have written the same thing.

- **Etymology and "also used as".** Both optional, both usually empty. The
  second is a chip rather than a picker, because "is this also used as an
  action?" is a question somebody either has an answer to instantly or does
  not - and the offers are per class, so changing the word class clears them
  rather than leaving a claim nobody can see they made.

- **Six classes are asked the agreement question.** As first built, only noun,
  proper-noun, verb and auxiliary-verb got a paradigm and the other twenty-one
  got nothing, so a quantifier was asked exactly what a preposition was asked.
  `AGREEING_CLASSES` - adjective, quantifier, numeral, determiner, article,
  pronoun - now get `agreeingOne` and `agreeingTwo`, phrased as *"use it with
  one word"* and *"now with a different word"*.

- **Two examples rather than named cells, deliberately.** Naming the cells means
  naming the classes, and nobody has written that inventory down for Kasem. An
  adjective table with invented cells would publish a structure this project
  made up and then collect data shaped to fit it.

- **`ideophone` is excluded, and that is the open question.** Gur ideophones
  commonly carry intensive and reduplicated forms and `lexical-kinds.ts` says as
  much itself - but no Kasem attestation exists for *what* varies, and inventing
  a paradigm for the largest poorly described class is exactly the failure this
  module exists to prevent. It goes in the moment somebody attests it.

- **One widget, both contribution paths.** `lexical_detail_fields.dart` is used
  by the guided queue and by the open contribution form, so the two cannot drift
  into collecting different things about the same word.

- **`PageStorageKey` on every field in that widget is load-bearing.** An
  `ExpansionTile` and the text fields inside it store to the same PageStorage
  identifier when neither carries a key, so the tile's `bool` and a field's
  scroll `double` collide and the form throws `type 'bool' is not a subtype of
  type 'double?'`. A test found it; on a phone it would have been a crash in the
  middle of somebody's contribution.

### New - the guided queue can hear the word

- **A recorder in the queue.** A dictionary entry's whole point is a sound, and
  for as long as the guided queue has been the main way words arrive it was the
  one contribution path that could only send text. The open form has had a
  recorder since the play button on a published entry stopped being a stub; the
  queue did not, so the archive was filling with words nobody can hear - asked
  of the very people who know how they are said.

- **A failed upload never costs the answer.** The take goes to Storage first and
  separately, because the callable is handed a path rather than bytes. An upload
  that times out on a village connection reports itself, hands back null, and
  the typed answer - the translation, the class, the paradigm - is sent without
  it. The take stays attached, so a member on a better signal can try again.

- **A recording in somebody else's folder is refused.** Storage rules stop a
  member *writing* into another member's prefix, but nothing stopped them
  *naming* one in the payload, and an answer that pointed a reviewer at another
  member's private file would be a disclosure the rules never saw.
  `parseWordTranslationInput(raw, uid)` checks the path against the caller's own
  prefix, and refuses media it has no uid to check rather than trusting it.

- **The take is cleared with the word.** A recording of somebody saying "boy" is
  an answer to *boy*; carrying it to the next word would publish the wrong sound
  on a word nobody would think to check.

### New - a word we never asked for

- **"Thought of a word we have not asked for?"** The queue is fifteen thousand
  English words in frequency order and it hands them out in that order. Somebody
  who thought of a word while answering - the word for a tool their father used,
  a word they had just realised was missing - had two options and both were bad:
  skip until it came round, which for a word at rank nine thousand is never, or
  leave the queue, find Contribute, find the dictionary offer and start from a
  cold screen. Most people did neither, and the word was lost.

- **It does not touch `wordQueue`.** That collection is the English backlog being
  worked through, and a Kasem word somebody volunteered is not a prompt for
  anybody. It is a contribution, and it goes to the same review desk as every
  other one. The queue keeps its place: this is a pushed route, and the word
  behind it is still there on the way back.

### Changed - a published entry reads as a paradigm, not a paragraph

- **The forms are a table.** They used to be joined with newlines into a single
  string - `With "the": bukam` then `Many: buga` - which is a table drawn with a
  colon. At three rows that was tolerable; at six it is a paragraph, and the one
  thing somebody scanning a paradigm does is run their eye down the *forms*
  column, which a run-on line does not have. Grouped as "As a thing", "As an
  action" and "Changes with the word it goes with".

- **The forms are selectable and the labels are not.** Copying "the boy" out of
  a dictionary is an ordinary thing to want. Copying the word "Many" is not.

- **One card for how the word is said.** The recording, the IPA and the prose
  guide are one question, and which of the three an entry carries is an accident
  of who contributed it - three separate cards would make a well-documented word
  look like three unrelated facts. The transcription sits above the prose guide,
  so the reader who came for it gets an exact answer from one line and everybody
  else reads the sentence underneath.

- **"Also used as" is a sentence, not a chip.** `verb` in a chip is a label a
  learner has to decode; "This word is also used as a verb. The forms above
  cover every way it is used." is the thing the chip was standing in for, and it
  costs one line.

- **Concord is reported, never concluded.** Where the recorded forms contain a
  known article or a known numeral series, the entry says *takes "kam" for
  "the"* and *counts with "balei"* - as an observation read off what a speaker
  typed. It does not say the word belongs to a class. Six forms of *two* are
  attested (balei, yalei, nlei, selei, telei, delei) and which noun takes which
  is precisely what nobody has established; harvesting a fully glossed chapter of
  Genesis produced **one** noun observed with both an article and a numeral in
  seventy clauses, which is why these are now collected from speakers directly.
  Renders nothing at all when neither matched - nothing, not a hedge, because an
  entry that says "class not established" on every row teaches a reader to stop
  reading the line.

- **The agreement pair is captioned as what it is.** "Two uses a speaker
  recorded. There may well be more forms than these two - which ones a word has,
  and what picks between them, has not been established for Kasem yet."

- **Prose fields refuse a number.** `ipa`, `kasemDefinition` and `etymology` are
  read by a reader that returns text or nothing, unlike the general one that
  stringifies. `ipa: 42` on a corrupt document would otherwise render as the
  pronunciation `/42/` - a false and unfalsifiable statement about how a Kasem
  word is said, on an entry a learner has no reason to doubt.

- **`definiteArticle`, `numeralSeries` and `numeralPrefix` are stored as
  descriptive readings.** They restate what a speaker typed so a reader does not
  have to re-parse it and a query can ask "which entries were counted with the
  ya- series" without scanning. They do **not** write `nounClass`, and
  `induceNounClass` does not read them. `kasemMorphology.test.mjs` -> "reading a
  marker never becomes inducing a class" is the guard on that separation.

### New - Explore has a bar of its own

- **A way out that is visible.** Explore is the one destination the shell draws
  no rail under - right, because full-bleed video with a tab bar painted over it
  is a tab with chrome on it rather than a place - but that left the only exit as
  a *gesture*: the system back swipe, which competes with the feed's own
  horizontal drags and which nothing on screen mentions. Somebody who opened
  Explore to look at one reel had no visible way back to the conversation they
  were reading.

- **Four destinations and a back button.** For you, Following, Post and Saved,
  icon over label, drawn on a gradient rather than a solid strip so the bar stays
  legible over a bright frame without becoming a black band across somebody's
  video. Search stays at the top left, because search is not a destination - it
  is a thing you do to the feed you are already in.

- **`kExploreNavBarHeight` is published, not private.** Every reel card reserves
  it under the caption and action rail, and the shell floats the connection
  banner above it. A bar whose height only the bar knows is a bar that covers the
  caption on the first phone with a different text scale.

- **The bar carries its own ink.** Explore's empty state is a bare `Stack` with
  no `Material` in it, so an `InkWell` reaching for an ancestor would work in the
  feed and throw on the empty state - which is precisely the screen a member is
  most likely to be navigating away from.

- **Drawn on the empty state too.** An empty Following feed is exactly where
  somebody needs the way back to For you and the way out of Explore. Until the
  bar existed, that screen had one button on it and no navigation at all.

- **The feed's chrome is a slot, not a fixture.** `ReelFeedView` takes a
  `footer` and a `bottomInset`, because three surfaces show that feed and only
  one of them has anywhere else to navigate to. Putting Explore's bar under a
  creator's own page would offer somebody looking at one creator a switch between
  For you and Following.

### Fixed - a reel that kept playing under everything

- **Sound over the search screen, the recorder and the saved list.** The feed
  decided whether to play from `ExploreScreen.isActive`, which is the *shell's*
  answer to "is Explore the selected tab" - and pushing a route does not change
  that. Explore is still the selected tab; it just has something on top of it. So
  tapping Search got you a keyboard, a list of results and a stranger's video
  talking over all of it, and tapping Create got the recorder's microphone
  competing with a clip you could no longer see.

- **Two different questions, now both asked.** `_overlayOpen` is held by the
  screen that pushes all three routes and therefore knows exactly when each
  returns - rather than by a `RouteAware`, which would need a navigator observer
  registered at app level for one screen's benefit. The feed plays only when
  Explore is selected *and* nothing it pushed is covering it.

### Fixed - the post Android threw away

- **The bug.** Opening the camera hands the screen to another activity, and on a
  phone with little memory to spare Android is entitled to destroy this whole
  process while it waits. It does, often - the camera is heavy and this app may
  be holding a video decoder and an image cache at the same moment. The member
  takes the picture, the camera finishes, and the app cold-starts with the post
  gone and the photograph gone with it.

- **Neither half was actually lost.** Android holds the result and hands it over
  exactly once on request; without that request it is discarded silently, every
  time. `CommunityMediaPicker.recoverLostMedia` is the request. The words are
  recovered separately, from a draft the composer writes down at the one moment
  it *knows* it is about to be suspended - the tap that opens the picker.

- **The fix cannot live in the composer, because by then the composer does not
  exist.** It runs from the screen that comes back up. The composer clears the
  draft in `dispose`, whichever way it closes - posted, cancelled, backed out
  of - so a draft still on disk at the next launch belongs to a composer that
  never got to run its own teardown, which is exactly and only the case this is
  for.

- **It reopens the composer rather than announcing anything.** Somebody who has
  just taken a photograph for a post is trying to finish the post; a banner
  offering to restore it is one more tap between them and the thing they were
  already doing. The reply or quote is re-fetched live rather than restored from
  the draft, because the post may have been edited or deleted while the app was
  gone and nobody should reply to writing that no longer says that.

- **Attachments are filtered against the disk.** A path the system has since
  cleaned out of the cache would reach the uploader as a missing file and fail
  the whole post. What survives is restored; what did not is dropped.

- **The recorder gets the same rescue, smaller.** `CreateReelScreen` asks for the
  lost clip on the way in and fills only an empty slot - a member who has already
  chosen a clip since the interruption chose it on purpose, and replacing it with
  an older take would be the app overruling them. No draft on disk is needed
  there, because that screen holds one clip and a caption nobody has usually
  typed yet.

### New - links from unverified accounts are shown, and dead

- **Masking, not a ban, and the reasoning is the feature.** A refusal teaches a
  scammer to write "wa dot me slash 233" instead, which no pattern can mask and
  every reader can still follow. Masking keeps the address visible and dead,
  which is the outcome that actually protects somebody.

- **The composer asks once and takes no for an answer.** An unverified member
  posting a link is offered verification - "One minute, and your links work" - or
  "Post without a live link". Choosing to verify keeps the draft up rather than
  publishing on the way back: somebody returning from a two-step flow should
  press Post themselves, because a post that appears because they finished an
  unrelated screen is a post they did not send.

- **The reader draws it struck through, with a line saying why.** And the link
  preview card is **suppressed outright** for such a post rather than drawn and
  made untappable - the card is the link's own headline, picture and pitch
  fetched from the page and reprinted on our timeline, and drawing it while
  declining to open it would hand a scam the most persuasive surface in the feed.

- **One pattern, three consumers.** `communityLinkPattern` in
  `community_links.dart` is used by the composer's gate, `PostText`'s renderer
  and the preview card. They used to disagree - the reader linkified only
  `https?://` while nothing gated anything - and the gap was exactly the move
  worth making: post `wa.me/233...`, be gated by nothing, and render as ordinary
  text a reader retypes by hand. If those ever diverge again, the shape that
  slips through is the shape somebody chose *because* it slips through.

- **Bare hosts match only a curated ending list** (`com net org ... me ly gh ng
  tk ml ga cf`). Deliberately conservative: a reader shown a warning on ordinary
  writing stops reading warnings.

- **The gate reads the live profile.** `authorMark` is the same value the badge
  beside the name uses. A stamp on the post is what the author's client wrote at
  the time, and somebody who verified this morning must not have last week's
  posts still masked. `VerifiedMark.none` is the only value meaning "nobody has
  checked"; every other mark already rests on a verified phone.

- **Not enforced in Firestore rules, on purpose.** Skipping is an allowed
  outcome, so there is nothing to refuse at write time.

### Changed - the corpus can be asked for counting

- **`numeral` is its own corpus tag**, not a corner of `plural`. The numeral is
  where Kasem shows a noun's class most plainly - six forms of *two*, selected by
  the noun being counted - and sentences that show one are the raw material for
  that question. Filed under `plural` they were buried among ordinary plurals,
  which is exactly the retrieval failure the tag list exists to prevent.

- **Kawuri quotes the forms an entry records**, worded for the briefing rather
  than as field names, and carries an explicit instruction never to build a form
  that is not listed. An entry with no forms recorded says nothing about forms.

### Not in the bundle - three scripts and a chapter

None of the following ships in the app. They are operator tooling and seed data,
listed because they were written alongside this release and are how its data got
where it is.

- `services/functions/scripts/respell-dictionary.mjs` - respells verified Burkina
  headwords to their Ghana form, pushing the original into `alternateKasemTerms`
  rather than discarding it, and moving `dialect` from Burkina reference to Ghana
  Kasem so the row does not assert a Ghana spelling under a Burkina label.
  Refuses any row whose Ghana spelling already exists as its own entry.
- `services/functions/scripts/seed-evidence.mjs` - 31 notes covering 70 clauses
  into `kasemEvidence` and `grammarNotes`, group `bible:genesis-1`, validated
  through the real `parseNote`.
- `services/functions/scripts/seed-gloss-words.mjs` - 125 dictionary
  contributions, written as a **pair**: `collectionContributions` (the member's
  receipt) and `submissions` (what the review desk actually reads). Writing only
  the first is invisible.
- `data/bible-seed/genesis-1.json` - the chapter map both seed scripts read.

Both seed scripts build their documents by importing the real builders from
`services/functions/lib/`, so a seeded row is shaped identically to one a member
typed, and all three scripts are dry run by default.

## Deploying this one

The backend for the advanced entry was deployed on 2026-09-06, twice - the second
time carrying the agreement slots - covering `submitWordTranslation`,
`submitCollectionContribution`, `decideSubmission` and `kawuriChat`. **That
deploy was not re-verified while cutting this bundle**, and the tree has since
gained the queue recording (`media` on `submitWordTranslation`) and the `numeral`
corpus tag. Treat the list below as required rather than done:

1. `npm run build:functions`
2. Regenerate `services/functions/functions.yaml` before deploying, or delete it
   if one has appeared. A stale manifest silently skips triggers.
3. `firebase deploy --only functions`
4. No Firestore rules change is needed. `dictionaryEntries` is written by the
   Admin SDK, and the link gate is deliberately not a rule - skipping
   verification is an allowed outcome, so there is nothing to refuse at write
   time.

**A limit worth naming.** Every new field is optional and silently dropped, so
there is no unauthenticated request that proves the *specific* new slots are
live: a probe proves the bundle boots and a string grep proves what was uploaded,
but nothing cheap distinguishes "deployed with the agreement slots" from
"deployed without". A staff-authenticated publish of a quantifier is the only
real end-to-end proof.

**Until this bundle is on a track, nothing a member holds sends the new fields.**
The deployed callables simply receive payloads without them, which is the case
they are written to accept.
