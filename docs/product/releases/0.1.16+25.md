The theme is narrower than last time and it is a single sentence: **the archive
can now correct itself.**

Every word in this dictionary was, until today, permanent in the one way that
matters. A validator who could see that an entry had a typo in its example
sentence had exactly one tool: reject the *next* contribution. The correction
path that existed asked them to contribute a fresh entry and wait for a second
validator to approve it - which produced a duplicate, needed two people for a
one-character fix, and left the wrong entry live in the meantime. In practice
nobody used it, and the archive kept its typos.

Duplicates were worse, because they arrive honestly. Two members who cannot see
each other's work enter the same word, both are reviewed carefully, both are
published, and now the dictionary holds the word twice with half the detail on
each. `firestore.rules` says `allow write: if false` on `dictionaryEntries`, so
there was no delete either - only an unpublish that leaves the pair sitting
there.

And the reviewer could not see the collision at the moment it mattered. The
review desk showed the submission and nothing at all about the archive it was
about to join.

The second half of the release is the screen this all happens on. The dictionary
was one scrolling list with a search box on top, in the middle of the file that
draws Music, Literature, Audiobooks and Video. That shape answers exactly one
question - *what is this word?* - and a learner who had met a word in its plural,
or who wanted to see what the archive holds under ŋ, or who spelled a word the
way it sounded, got nothing.

## Full change list

### New - a published word can be corrected

- **`editDictionaryEntry`.** A validator or admin opens a published entry, fixes
  it, and republishes. No second contribution, no second reviewer, no window
  where the wrong entry is the live one.

- **Only what somebody changed is sent.** `EntryPatch.withField` compares each
  box against the value it opened with and drops the ones that match; the server
  reads an absent key as "leave it alone". A validator who came to fix a gloss
  cannot blank the etymology a different person spent an afternoon on. This is
  the safety property of the whole screen and a test holds it.

- **The senses are the sharpest case, and they are opted in.** A sense may carry
  four example sentences and the form shows one pair of boxes, so the senses
  enter the patch *only* when a reviewer actually types in one of them. Sending
  them unconditionally would silently drop the second, third and fourth sentence
  off every entry somebody merely opened.

- **Provenance is not editable at all.** `contributorId`, `sourceContribution`,
  `createdAt`, `approvedBy`, `audioUrl` and `homographIndex` are not in
  `EDITABLE_FIELDS`, so no patch can reach them. The person who gave the word
  keeps the credit for it however many times a reviewer tidies the spelling, and
  nothing can reassign authorship or renumber a citation.

- **A headword cannot be cleared.** An entry with no headword cannot be looked
  up, cannot be said aloud and is dropped on read by the phone - so blanking one
  would withdraw a word nobody asked to withdraw. The editor refuses it and
  points at the Published switch instead.

- **Respelling is the one case that renumbers, and it must be.**
  `kasem-homographs.ts` is emphatic that a homograph number is an identity and
  is never reassigned. Respelling is the case it was not written for: `mo²`
  respelled to `mɔ` is no longer one of the words written `mo`, and keeping the
  2 leaves a superscript pointing into a series the entry has left while the
  group it has joined may already have a `mɔ²`. So a respelling that changes the
  grouping key takes the next free number in the new group, and the number it
  vacated is never handed out again. Capitalisation and spacing change nothing.

### New - two entries for one word can be folded into one

- **`mergeDictionaryEntries`.** The kept entry keeps every answer it already has
  and gains only the ones it was missing. That is the only default under which
  merging the wrong pair - or the right pair the wrong way round - is
  recoverable, because nothing the target said has been lost.

- **Lists and the paradigm union rather than choosing.** Two members who each
  recorded one half of a noun's paradigm - one gave "the boy", the other "two
  boys" - have between them recorded exactly the thing the merge was worth doing
  for. Picking a side would throw one of them away. Senses append,
  de-duplicated on the definition, so merging the same pair twice is a no-op.

- **Conflicts are the reviewer's, field by field.** The compare screen draws
  every field either side filled, marks the ones where both said something and
  they differ, and lists what moves across without being asked about. "Merge" is
  a word people are right to be nervous of, and the list of exactly what arrives
  is what makes the button pressable.

- **Which entry stays is a swap away, and the screen says why.** The kept entry
  keeps its document id, and therefore every saved word, shared `/entry/…` link,
  Kawuri citation and note a learner wrote. That is usually the older one *even
  when the newer one has the better text* - the text moves across, the identity
  cannot.

- **The duplicate is retired, not deleted.** It is unpublished and marked
  `mergedInto`, and `/entry/<old id>` now draws a short page naming the word it
  became. Deleting it would leave a dangling id behind every one of those
  pointers, and "this entry could not be loaded" reads as *the dictionary lost
  this word* rather than *these were one word all along*.

- **`deleteDictionaryEntry` exists and is admin-only.** For a row that should
  never have been written - a test entry, a paste into the wrong box. The whole
  document is copied into `auditLogs` first, so a deletion in error can be
  reconstructed by somebody who knows it happened. The homograph number it held
  stays spent.

- **Every one of these needs a reason of ten characters or more.** The audit
  entry is the only record of why a published word changed, and "updated" tells
  a future reader nothing they could not already see.

### New - the reviewer is told before they decide

- **The review desk asks the archive first.** A dictionary contribution now runs
  `findDictionaryEntryMatches` and draws the answer *above* the work itself,
  with each existing entry's meaning, word class, sense number, dialect and
  whether it carries a recording. A reviewer who reads a definition carefully,
  checks the consent, publishes, and leaves a second copy of a word the archive
  already holds has done all the work and still made the archive worse.

- **It prompts and never blocks.** Two entries under one spelling is a
  homograph - `mo¹` the particle beside `mo²` the focus marker - and a desk that
  refused the second would make the dictionary unable to hold a distinction the
  language makes.

- **"Merge" from that banner publishes first, then folds.** In that order,
  because a submission is not a dictionary entry and the id the merge needs does
  not exist until the publish writes it. The contributor is credited on the
  record exactly as they would have been.

- **Near misses are reported as near, with a percentage.** The comparison is
  edit distance over the case-folded spelling with a floor of 0.7, so a
  one-letter slip in a four-letter word is offered and a one-letter difference
  in a two-letter word is not.

- **And it is deliberately *not* the search folding.** `foldForSearch` folds ɩ
  to i so a learner who cannot type ɩ still finds the word. Doing that here
  would report `dɩ` and `di` as the same entry and invite a reviewer to merge
  two different lexemes and call it tidying up. A test pins that.

### Changed - the dictionary is a screen, not a channel

- **Three destinations.** **Look up** - the search. **Browse** - the whole
  archive in Kasem alphabetical order with a letter rail down the side, because
  you cannot look up a word you have not met and browsing is how vocabulary is
  actually acquired. **Saved** - the words this reader kept, until now visible
  only from elsewhere in the app.

- **The rail lists only letters the archive has words under.** A rail offering
  `q` on a Kasem dictionary sends a reader to an empty screen and reads as a
  broken index rather than as an honest gap. It grows on its own as the
  dictionary does.

- **Three destinations, and not five.** Flashcards, tone drills and a document
  reader are the obvious next ones and none is here, because each needs data the
  archive does not yet hold in quantity - a study system built over an
  incomplete lexicon teaches the gaps.

- **`DictionaryCollectionScreen` survives as a four-line forwarder.** Two
  screens push it by that name and the Collection grid is where a member looks
  for the dictionary.

### Changed - search assumes the reader knows less than the archive does

The assumption throughout is that the person searching does not know how the
word is spelled, does not know its tone, cannot type half its letters, and may
well have met it in a form the dictionary does not file it under.

- **A plural finds its singular, and the row says why.** Type `biə`, get `bu`,
  and read *"biə is the plural of bu"*. The definite, plural-definite and
  counted forms resolve the same way, as do the verb tenses. This is the Kasem
  substitution for Pleco's stroke-order diagrams: spend the interface on what is
  structurally hard about the language, which here is noun class morphology. A
  learner who meets `biə` has no reliable way to guess the singular, and a
  conventional dictionary leaves them stranded at exactly that point.

- **The explanation is drawn only where it is needed.** A headword match is
  never explained, because a note on every row is a note nobody reads.

- **Wildcards.** `ba*ra` and `b?` for a half-remembered shape. Anchored to the
  whole word, because a reader typing a wildcard has described the whole word
  and an unanchored match would bury it under everything containing the
  fragment. Everything except the two wildcards is escaped, so a query with a
  bracket searches for a bracket rather than becoming a regular expression a
  public search box has to survive.

- **"Did you mean" instead of an empty screen.** An empty result says *this word
  is not in the dictionary*, which for a learner working from something they
  heard is usually false - the word is there and they spelled it as it sounded.

- **Scope and filters.** Kasem / English / both, and narrowing to entries with a
  recording or with an example, plus the four word classes anybody filters by.
  There is deliberately no filter by semantic domain: it is filled on the small
  minority of entries contributed since senses existed, and a filter that
  silently hides nine tenths of the archive teaches a reader it is smaller than
  it is.

- **Ranking breaks ties on completeness.** With a few thousand entries a bad
  ordering puts the right answer on the second screen, where nobody looks.
  Between two entries that answer equally well, the one a learner can hear and
  see used wins.

- **The cap is stated.** Fifty rows, with "Showing 50 of 312" underneath. A list
  silently truncated tells a reader the dictionary is small.

### Fixed - a tone mark made a word unfindable

`foldForSearch` stripped combining marks but not precomposed ones. A headword
stored as `bù` - which is what a paste from a document, an iOS keyboard or any
NFC-normalised import produces - was reachable by typing `bù` and by nothing
else. That is a word only somebody who already knows the tone can look up, which
is the exact failure the whole file exists to prevent.

Thirty precomposed vowels now fold to their base letter. Dart has no Unicode
normalisation in its core library and this is a five-vowel language, so the
table is written out rather than a dependency added for ten entries.

### New - the Kasem letters, above the keyboard

- **ɛ ə ɣ ɩ ŋ ɔ ʋ**, with a capitals toggle, on the search box and on every
  Kasem field of the editor. 785 of the published headwords carry at least one
  of them: ɩ in 640, ʋ in 195, ə in 177, ɔ in 156, ŋ in 115, ɛ in 9.

- **Folding rescues the reader; this rescues the writer.** A learner who types
  `di` for `dɩ` has been handled since the folding shipped. A contributor or a
  validator who cannot produce the real letter files the word under a spelling
  that is a different word, and no amount of folding helps them.

- **It appears only while a Kasem field has the cursor**, and only over the
  Kasem fields. Inserting `ɛ` into the English gloss is never what anybody meant,
  and a permanently visible bar is a permanently stolen inch of a 720p screen.

- **This is the substitution for Pleco's handwriting panel**, not an addition to
  the Kasem keyboard that shipped in 0.1.15. That is a system-wide input method;
  this is seven keys inside this app, for the member who has not installed it.

### Changed - the result row answers as completely as a row can

- Headword with its sense number where one is owed, IPA beside it, word class,
  a speaker icon where there is a recording, and **every** meaning numbered
  rather than the first plus a count. The count of an answer is not the answer,
  and three short glosses fit on two lines.

- An absent recording draws no icon, a one-meaning entry draws one meaning, and
  nothing anywhere says "no example yet".

### Changed - the entry screen

- **An "Edit" pencil for the roles the backend will accept.** Offered only to
  validators, reviewers and admins; a pencil that always ends in "validator
  access is required" teaches a member the app is broken rather than that the
  action is not theirs.

- **A withdrawn entry no longer claims to be published.** Staff read the
  document directly now, so the status pill says NOT PUBLISHED where that is the
  truth - and a validator who withdraws an entry from the editor can still reach
  it to put it back.

### Also new since the 0.1.15 bundle

These were committed after the 0.1.15 bundle was built and ship here for the
first time.

- **Signing in now leads somewhere** (`3ede844`). Signing in with Google closed
  a card and put the member back where they came from with no handle, no name
  the community knew them by, no mark and an empty feed - joining looked
  identical to nothing happening. There is now a five-step flow for an account
  with no `communityProfiles` record: a welcome, the Kassena name, the profile,
  the phone, and people to follow. Only the handle is compulsory, because it is
  what the community is keyed on and cannot be changed afterwards.

- **A birthday, month and day, never a year.** A profile is world-readable, so a
  full date of birth on one is the most useful field an impersonator could take;
  the day answers the only question the community has. Validated in the rules,
  and the day list narrows as the month is chosen so 31 April is never offered.

- **The Kassena name request button says what is missing.** It held itself off
  until the "who bears this name?" box had ten characters and explained that
  nowhere.

- **The determiner decides the pronoun** (`1cb0342`). All eight determiners now
  carry the pronoun a speaker gave for them - wom→o, kam→ka, dem→de, sem→se,
  kom→ko, tem→te, bam→ba, yam→ya. Stored as a table rather than derived by
  trimming the `-m`, because `wom` is the one row a string operation would get
  wrong. Neither client prefills the box: a prefilled answer is accepted without
  being read, and the archive would then hold a derivation indistinguishable
  from an attestation.

## Known gaps

- **Nothing here is committed.** The bundle was built from a dirty working tree
  over `3ede844`. That is a step back from 0.1.15, which was the first bundle
  built from a pushed commit, and it should be corrected before this AAB is
  uploaded - a release whose source cannot be checked out is a release nobody
  can reproduce or bisect.

- **The editor and merge screens are mobile only.** The review desk is, so this
  follows it. The callables are surface-agnostic, so adding the same to
  TribeStudio or the admin console is client work only.

- **None of the new dictionary screens has a widget test.** The search library
  underneath them has 25, and the ranking, folding, wildcards, morphology and
  suggestions are all held there - but nothing pumps a frame of the editor, the
  merge screen or the three destinations. An editor that can rewrite a published
  word deserves one before it is used in anger.

- **The merge and edit callables have no emulator test.** Their pure half is
  covered 25 ways; the transactions, the role checks, the renumbering query and
  the audit writes are covered by reading. `firebase/tests/creatorFunctions.test.mjs`
  is the pattern to follow and this is the first thing to add.

- **The duplicate check has never run against the real archive.** It is built
  and tested against fixtures. The live collection has rows with no
  `headwordKey`, which is exactly the population the four-query gather exists
  for, and the first real use will be the first evidence that it reaches them.

- **The letter rail jumps to an estimated offset**, not a measured one. The rows
  are near-uniform so it lands within a row, and measuring would mean a
  `GlobalKey` on every entry to save a reader one flick. Worth revisiting if the
  rows ever become variable height.

- **There is a stale AAB at a second path.**
  `apps/mobile/android/app/production/release/app-production-release.aab` is a
  gitignored leftover from 2026-08-26, 76.1 MB, and it is not this release.
  Every release note points at `build/app/outputs/bundle/productionRelease/`
  and that is the one to upload; the other path is old enough to be a genuine
  trap and should probably just be deleted.

- **Nothing has been run on a device.** This build has not been installed. The
  Kasem key bar's placement above the keyboard, the nav bar, and the letter rail
  on a 720p screen are the three things that most want a real phone.

- **Carried from 0.1.15 and still open:** the digraph shortcuts on the system
  keyboard are still a draft and want review with fluent speakers; the keyboard
  has no key-press popup preview; edge-to-edge has not been checked on a
  physical Android 15 device; and the TribeStudio dictionary desk has still not
  been visually confirmed in a browser.
