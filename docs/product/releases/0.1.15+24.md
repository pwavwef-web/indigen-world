---

One theme, and it is the same one this project keeps arriving at from different
directions: **say all of it.**

A word was allowed one meaning. Not one *word* for its meaning - one meaning.
English *toy* is a plaything, a trinket and a small breed of dog before it is a
verb, and the form asked once, took the answer, and stored "toy, plaything,
small dog" as a single meaning that happened to contain commas. The example
sentence somebody typed underneath belonged to none of the three, and a learner
reading it had no way to know which one it was showing them.

And the keyboard built to type this language could not type this language. It
had a key for ɛ, which appears in nine headwords, and no way at all to produce
ɩ, which appears in six hundred and forty.

Both are the same failure: a thing that looks finished, is in front of people,
and quietly cannot hold what it was built to hold.

## Full change list

### New - a dictionary entry that can mean more than one thing

- **`senses`, on the record.** Several meanings per entry, each carrying its own
  `definition`, `examples`, `register`, `domain`, `kasemDefinition`,
  `usageNote`, `synonyms` and `antonyms` - and its own `partOfSpeech`, for the
  word that is a noun in its first meanings and a verb in a later one. Defined
  once in `packages/contracts/schemas/lexical-entry.schema.json` and mirrored in
  `services/functions/src/lexical-senses.ts`,
  `apps/mobile/lib/domain/entry_sense.dart` and
  `apps/tribestudio/src/creator/lexicon.ts`; all four carry each other's paths
  and must agree.

- **A sense is not a homograph, and the code says so in four places.**
  `homographIndex` numbers entries that are *different words* sharing a spelling
  - `mo¹` the focus particle beside `mo²` - and those keep separate documents
  and a number that is never reassigned, because a citation whose target moves
  is worse than no citation. `senses` are the several meanings of *one* word:
  one document, one headword, one etymology. Both render as "1." and "2." on the
  page and only one of them is a stable identity. Conflating them is the
  commonest way a dictionary schema goes wrong and it would have been very easy
  to do here.

- **Nothing was migrated, and nothing had to be.** `sensesOrLegacy` on the
  server and `DictionaryEntry.displaySenses` on the phone lift a legacy entry's
  flat gloss and single sentence into one sense *on read*. No stored row was
  rewritten and an entry approved last year still publishes byte-for-byte as it
  did. The consequence worth stating: a screen written against `displaySenses`
  renders the entire archive correctly the day it ships, including the fifteen
  thousand entries nobody is ever going to re-contribute.

- **And nothing older breaks.** `englishText` stays the flat summary line every
  existing reader consults, and the publication projection now also writes
  `englishTranslations` - the split list, which `collection_data.dart` already
  read first and which nothing had ever written. So a build that has never heard
  of senses shows "plaything, trinket, small dog" exactly as it always did, and
  a build that has shows three meanings with their own sentences underneath.

- **A single bare sense is refused storage.** `sensesAddDetail` declines to
  write an array whose one entry says exactly what `englishText` already says.
  Otherwise fifteen thousand rows would grow an array to repeat one string.

- **Register and subject field are closed lists, not free tags.** Free text
  produces "farming", "farm", "Farming" and "agric" on four entries that mean
  the same thing, which defeats the one query the field exists to serve. The
  labels are deliberately not the lexicographic abbreviations - "colloq." is a
  vocabulary a contributor has to be taught, and `avoided` is called *"not said
  in front of elders"* because that is the thing a speaker will actually tell
  you.

- **Synonyms are stored as words, not as entry ids.** The word a speaker offers
  as a synonym very often has no entry yet, and storing ids would mean refusing
  to record the answer until it did - losing exactly the vocabulary the
  cross-reference was pointing at, and losing it silently. The renderer resolves
  what it can: a synonym with an entry becomes a chip that opens it, one without
  stays plain text, and the two look different.

### Changed - the contribution form asks for meanings, plural

- **"This word means something else too."** The form's single "what does it mean
  in English" box is now a section that owns every meaning, with add, remove and
  reorder. The obvious cheaper design - keep the old box for meaning one, offer
  a second section for the rest - was rejected: the first sense would then have
  been the only one that could not carry a register, a subject field or its own
  sentence, and every entry's *first* meaning would have been systematically
  poorer than its second.

- **`title` is now derived, not typed.** The pipeline reads `title` as the
  English side of the record, so it is kept in step with the first meaning
  rather than being a second field somebody has to keep in agreement. Everything
  downstream - the draft check, the validator, the callable, the review desk -
  goes on reading `title` and never learns it is typed into a different box.

- **The example boxes moved into the meaning they illustrate.** That was always
  the question they were trying to ask. A single pair at the bottom of the form
  could only ever illustrate one of several meanings, and never said which.

- **The two legacy example fields are still sent.** Filled from the first
  meaning that has a sentence, because they are read by the review desk and by
  every app build that predates senses. The entry screen suppresses the
  duplicate at render time instead - `hasSenseExamples` - so no sentence is
  printed twice under two headings.

- **The median word still costs zero extra taps.** One meaning, typed into the
  one box that is open when the screen loads, and sent. The second meaning, the
  register, the subject field, the Kasem gloss, the usage note and the
  cross-references are all behind a disclosure that starts closed.

- **The guided queue is deliberately left single-sense.** It prompts with one
  English word and asks what the Kasem for it is, so the answer is one meaning
  by construction. A sense editor there would cost fifteen thousand queued words
  a question almost none of them need.

### Changed - the entry screen shows the meanings apart

- **Numbered senses, grouped by word class, entry's own class first.** The one
  convention every printed dictionary shares, because it answers "is this the
  noun or the verb?" before a reader has to read a definition to find out. A
  word with one class draws no class headings at all, so the common entry is not
  made to look complicated in order to serve the rare one.

- **Numbering runs across the entry, not within each group.** A learner citing
  "sense 3" means the third meaning of the word; numbering that restarted at the
  verbs would put two senses called 1 on one screen.

- **The number is drawn only when there is more than one.** A lone "1." above
  the only definition an entry has is a list marker for a list of one.

- **Screen readers get the sentence, not the digit.** The number chip is
  excluded from semantics and the definition is announced as *"Meaning 2 of 3.
  rain"*, because "1 water 2 rain" runs the number into the meaning.

- **A legacy entry never draws the block.** `hasStructuredSenses` asks the narrow
  question - did a contributor actually separate the meanings, and give one of
  them a label or a sentence - so the archive's comma-separated glosses are not
  printed twice on one screen.

### Changed - search reads every meaning

- **A word that appears only in a later sense is findable at all now.**
  `translations` carries the flat summary line; a sense's own Kasem gloss, its
  usage note and its synonyms are not in it, and those are exactly the words
  somebody reaches for when they half-remember a meaning.

- **Sense hits rank below every direct hit.** `kSenseMatchRank` is 6, one below
  the weakest rank `searchRank` itself returns, so a word whose headword matches
  always outranks a word that merely mentions the query in a note four senses
  down.

- **The extended letters fold inside senses too**, so a learner who cannot type
  ɩ and types `wia` still finds the note that says `wɩa`.

### Fixed - the Kasem keyboard could not type Kasem

Reviewed in full in `docs/product/kasem-keyboard-review.md`.

- **ɩ, ʋ and ə could not be produced at all.** No key, no gesture, no symbol
  page. Against this project's own counts in `kasem_orthography.dart` that is ɩ
  in 640 headwords, ʋ in 195 and ə in 177 - while ɛ, which had a dedicated key,
  appears in 9. The workaround it left is the one that does damage: type `i` for
  `ɩ` and the word is filed under a spelling that is a **different word**, which
  is precisely what `headwordKey` refuses to fold. Every extended letter now has
  its own key on a row of its own, always visible in Kasem mode, because
  reachability through an undiscoverable gesture is not reachability.

- **Long-press drew no hint.** ɛ and ɔ were reachable by holding `e` and `o` and
  nothing on screen said so - the only trace was a `contentDescription` only a
  screen reader hears. Every key with an alternate now shows it as a small
  superscript in the corner, as a `SpannableString` so it costs no layout.

- **The keyboard rebuilt itself on every keystroke.** `commit()` called
  `drawKeyboard()` whenever shift was in its one-shot state, and that tears down
  and re-allocates roughly forty Buttons and a hundred and twenty drawables. So
  typing any capitalised sentence - which is every sentence, because
  `shouldStartShifted` turns shift on for `CAP_SENTENCES` - rebuilt the whole
  keyboard between the tap and the letter. `updateLetterCase()` now relabels the
  sixteen views whose text actually changed.

- **Backspace did not repeat.** Clearing a mistyped sentence was forty taps. It
  repeats at 55 ms while held, with a longer first delay so a deliberate single
  delete never becomes two, and the repeat is cancelled in `onFinishInputView`
  so a held key cannot delete into whatever is opened next.

- **The declared language was a different language.** The subtype said
  `kss_GH`; ISO 639-3 `kss` is Southern Kisi, a Mel language of Sierra Leone and
  Liberia. Kasem is `xsm` - as `packages/contracts/schemas/common.schema.json`
  already states and as every submission already carries. Android matches on
  this when deciding which keyboard to offer for a field. Now `xsm_GH` with an
  explicit `languageTag="xsm-GH"`.

- **The settings link went somewhere misleading, and is gone.** It pointed at
  `MainActivity`; Android launches a settings activity with a plain `ACTION_MAIN`
  intent, indistinguishable from a launcher tap, so the gear beside "Kasem
  keyboard" in Android's settings dropped people on the app's home screen with
  no explanation and no way back. A link that goes somewhere misleading is worse
  than no link. The keyboard's settings are in-app, where the setup flow already
  sends people.

- **Keys were 47dp**, one below Android's 48dp minimum touch target, with no
  landscape adaptation - so sideways, five rows of fixed-height keys pushed the
  field being typed into off the screen. 48dp portrait, 38dp landscape.

- **The keyboard was always paper-white.** It now follows
  `UI_MODE_NIGHT_MASK`, re-read on `onStartInputView`.

- **Auto-capitals fired in password, email and URI fields**, where the first
  letter is significant and a capital is wrong. Those variations are excluded.

- **Enter ignored `IME_FLAG_NO_ENTER_ACTION`**, which is how a multi-line message
  box sends on every attempted line break. The flag is honoured before the
  action.

- **The language switch reset itself between fields**, so somebody who switched
  to English to type a URL was back in Kasem on the next field. The stored
  preference is now the language the keyboard *opens* in, not one that
  reasserts itself.

- **The bottom row sat under the gesture bar.** An IME's input view is one of
  our app's windows, so it inherits our target SDK - and from Android 15 a
  window belonging to an app targeting SDK 35 or above is laid out edge to
  edge. The space bar and the enter key were sharing their lower half with the
  system's own swipe target, and the tap went to whichever won.
  `applyNavigationBarInset` now pads the keyboard root by the `navigationBars`
  inset. Written against the platform API so it costs no dependency, and safe in
  both directions: where the system already insets the IME window the inset
  reports zero and the padding is exactly what it was. Found because Play
  Console raised edge-to-edge against 0.1.14 (23) - see below.

### New - the TribeStudio dictionary desk (web, already deployed)

Not in this bundle - it is at `https://tribestudio.web.app/studio/dictionary`
and went live with `8ec5936` - but it is the other half of the same change and
belongs in the same notes.

- **Two columns: the entry being written, and the entry as a learner will see
  it**, from the same fields in the same order, following the same rule that a
  section with nothing in it is not drawn. A contributor filling in eleven
  paradigm slots otherwise has no idea what any of it looks like.

- **A Kasem letter palette.** 785 of 1200 published entries carry a letter no
  desk keyboard produces. Without it the workaround is the same one the phone
  keyboard forced, with the same consequence.

- **Duplicate and homograph warning while typing**, which warns and never
  blocks - two entries under one spelling is a homograph, not an error, and 478
  of the 1200 published entries share a spelling with another.

- **Draft autosave**, because a four-sense entry with a paradigm is twenty
  minutes of work and one stray reload. Consent is deliberately never restored:
  it is an act, not a preference.

- **A completeness meter that guides and never gates.** A contributor who knows
  a word means "bottle" and nothing else has given the archive something real,
  and a form that refused it until they invented an etymology would trade a true
  small record for a false large one. `validate-studio.mjs` asserts that nothing
  in the meter can disable the send button.

- **It submits through `submitCollectionContribution`** - the same callable,
  review desk, homograph numbering and published document as a phone
  contribution. Writing `lexicalEntries` directly, the way the old workspace
  does, would have meant two review queues and two ideas of what a valid entry
  is.

## On the Play Console's edge-to-edge warning

Play Console raised *"Edge-to-edge may not display for all users"* against
0.1.14 (23), advising `enableEdgeToEdge()` for Kotlin or `EdgeToEdge.enable()`
for Java. It was worth taking seriously and it turned out to be two different
things wearing one label.

**On the Activity, there is nothing to fix, and the check cannot see why.**
Flutter already uses `SystemUiMode.edgeToEdge` by default - its own
`system_chrome.dart` says so outright: *"If your app targets Android SDK 15
(API 35), then your Flutter app uses SystemUiMode.edgeToEdge by default"*. So
the window is already edge to edge and `MediaQuery.padding` already carries the
insets, which the app already reads:

- `frosted_nav_bar.dart:161` offsets the rail by `MediaQuery.paddingOf(context).bottom`;
- `app_shell.dart` positions the profile orb at `MediaQuery.paddingOf(context).top + 6`;
- `music_overlay.dart` distinguishes `padding.bottom` from `viewPadding.bottom` deliberately;
- 35 files use `SafeArea`.

Play's check is a static scan for two specific androidx calls. A Flutter app has
no reason to make either, and adding `enableEdgeToEdge()` on top of Flutter's
own handling would be a second correction stacked on the first - it would also
fight the `AnnotatedRegion<SystemUiOverlayStyle>` this app uses to flip the
status bar icons between Explore and everything else. So the warning is left
standing deliberately. It is advisory, not a policy block.

**On the input method, the warning was right**, and it found a real bug - see
the last keyboard bullet above. That is the half of this that was genuinely
broken, and it was broken in code written the same day.

The honest position: the Activity is fine and is now evidenced rather than
assumed; the IME is fixed; neither has been confirmed on a physical Android 15
device with gesture navigation, which is the one thing that would settle it.

## Known gaps

- **The Play privacy policy must declare the input method** before this goes to
  review: that the keyboard processes text locally, stores nothing and sends
  nothing. It is true of the code today; it has to be said in the listing. This
  is the one item that blocks submission and it is not a code change.
- **The digraph shortcuts are still a draft.** Ch, Kw, Gw, Pw, Ŋw and the key
  positions need review with fluent Kasem speakers. The alphabet itself is now
  settled. Do not describe the layout as final.
- **The keyboard has no key-press popup preview.** Standard on commercial
  keyboards and the usual complaint on a small screen. Worth doing; not worth
  blocking on.
- **The studio dictionary page has not been visually confirmed in a browser.**
  It typechecks, builds, passes `validate-studio.mjs` and its chunk serves 200
  in production, but the authenticated view was never rendered on screen during
  development - a sign-in popup blocked automation and driving sign-in was not
  an option. Worth a look before it is announced to contributors.
- **Edge-to-edge has not been checked on a real Android 15 device.** The
  reasoning above is from the Flutter SDK source and from the app's own inset
  code, not from a screen. A gesture-navigation phone on Android 15 would settle
  both halves in about a minute, and it is the first thing to do with this
  build.
- **The keyboard's own changes have no instrumented test.** Kotlin IME behaviour
  is verified by compilation and by reading; `kasem_keyboard_screen_test.dart`
  covers only the Flutter settings page. An emulator test of key output would be
  the right next thing.
