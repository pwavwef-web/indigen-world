0.1.16 shipped with a known gap written at the bottom of its own release note:
*"Nothing has been run on a device... the Kasem key bar's placement above the
keyboard, the nav bar, and the letter rail on a 720p screen are the three things

It went on a phone. The key bar's placement was the first thing to break, and it
broke completely: **tapping any Kasem field in the word editor covered the
entire form**. Not a misalignment - a wall of ceiling-to-floor lines with the
seven letters stranded halfway down it, painted over the app bar as well as the
form.

Every fix here is something a phone found in an afternoon and no test in the
suite could have seen, because the suite tested what the widgets *do* and all
three were about how large they *are*, or where a sentence was written. They are
now held by tests that measure.

## Full change list

### Fixed - the Kasem letter bar covered the screen it was helping with

- **The bar is a strip along the bottom again.** `_Key` drew its glyph in a
  `Container` with an `alignment` and no height bound. A `Container` with an
  alignment grows to fill whatever height it is offered, and what a Scaffold
  offers its `bottomNavigationBar` is the height of the whole screen. So seven
  keys laid themselves out full-height; the Scaffold could not fit an
  800-pixel bar above the keyboard, applied its own `max(0, bottom - height)`
  rule and pinned the bar to `y=0`; and because the bottom bar is painted after
  the app bar, the result covered everything.

- **What that looked like** is worth recording, because it does not look like a
  layout bug. The key *borders* became seven vertical lines running the height
  of the display, the letters sat at the vertical centre of those lines, and the
  form was simply gone. Nothing in `lib/` that anybody had edited was involved.

- **The bar and each key now state their height** (`_kKeyHeight`, 40), in both
  places, with the reason written beside each. The community composer's bar had
  never had this problem because it has always been wrapped in a
  `SizedBox(height: 52)` - the same defence, arrived at by accident.

- **This was not only the editor.** The dictionary's Look up tab mounts the same
  bar above its nav rail, so focusing the search box did the same thing there.
  One fix, both screens.

### Fixed - the capital key was a lock, not a shift

- **One press meant capitals for the rest of the session.** The `AB` key
  latched. A key labelled "AB" invites exactly one exploratory press, and after
  it every letter the bar typed was a capital, with nothing but the row's own
  glyphs to say why. Reported from the field as "the Kasem keyboard sometimes
  types only capital letters", which is precisely what a silent latch looks like
  from the outside.

- **It is now a shift.** One capital, then it disarms itself - the way the shift
  on the keyboard below it behaves. Kasem takes a capital at the start of a
  sentence and on a name, which is one letter at a time; nobody types a headword
  in capitals, so a lock had no case to answer for.

- **It also disarms on leaving the Kasem boxes**, which closes the one remaining
  way an armed shift could outlive the letter it was meant for.

- **And it is legible now.** An up-arrow that fills with the accent colour while
  armed, instead of a two-letter label that showed the mode you were about to
  switch *to*.

### Fixed - deleting a word asked for a reason with nowhere to write it

- **The demand and the box are now in the same view.** The bin is in the app
  bar. The reason box was at the foot of a form several screens long, and the
  refusal - "Deleting needs a reason on the record. Write it first." - was
  written into an error line beside that box, below the fold, where the person
  who had just tapped the bin never saw it. What that produced in the field was
  somebody tapping every field on the screen looking for the place to write the
  reason they had been told was required.

- **`DeleteReasonPrompt`** is the confirmation and the reason box together.
  Delete stays disabled until there are ten characters in it - the same floor
  `deleteDictionaryEntry` enforces, checked on the phone so the button is
  visibly unavailable rather than failing on the server with the entry still on
  screen - and it counts down the characters still needed.

- **Anything already written in the form's own reason box is carried across**,
  so a reviewer who wrote their reason there first does not write it twice, and
  a delete that fails on the way out leaves the words where they can be edited
  and tried again.

- **Save cannot move into a dialog** - saving is the form's own purpose - so it
  does the other half instead: a refused save scrolls to the box it is
  complaining about, focuses it, and says the refusal in a toast as well as in
  the error line. Same bug, both paths.

- **Worth naming:** with the bar covering the form, the whole editor was
  invisible the moment any Kasem field took focus. That is very likely half of
  why the reason box could not be found either. Two bugs, one symptom.

### New - the keyboard shows the key under your finger

- On a 720p phone a key is about eight millimetres wide and a thumb is wider, so
  at the moment of the press the one person who cannot see which key was hit is
  the person pressing it. Every stock keyboard floats the letter above the
  finger; this one now does too.

- It matters more here than on a QWERTY keyboard, because the letters this
  keyboard exists for are the ones nobody has muscle memory for - `ɩ` beside
  `ɪ`, `ʋ` beside `v`, and a long-press alternate printed in superscript in the
  corner of the same key. Typing `dɩ` and getting `di` is a word the dictionary
  cannot match, and without a preview the first sign of it is the wrong word on
  screen.

- Only on the character keys. Shift, backspace, enter and the language switch
  change state rather than emit a letter, and floating their glyph over the row
  would say nothing about what they did - which is why the stock keyboards do
  not preview them either. The glyph is read at press time, so it follows the
  shift rather than showing the case that was current when the keyboard was
  drawn.

## The gaps 0.1.16 listed, and what happened to them

0.1.16 closed with nine known gaps. Seven are closed, one is narrowed to the
single check only a person can do, and one needs a physical device.

### Closed - the bundle is checkoutable

Committed as `40a8af3` and pushed before the build. See the header.

### Closed - the release build trap has a fix rather than a warning

`npm run build:mobile-aab` (`scripts/build-mobile-aab.mjs`) deletes
`android/app/src/main/java/io/flutter/plugins/GeneratedPluginRegistrant.java`
before building. That file is generated and gitignored; a test run writes one
registering `integration_test`, a dev dependency; and a release build reusing it
fails with `package dev.flutter.plugins.integration_test does not exist`, which
names nothing anybody wrote and only ever happens when a release follows a test
run - the order a release goes in.

It was not a hypothetical this time. The script's first line of output on this
build was:

```
· Removed a GeneratedPluginRegistrant.java that registered integration_test.
```

The runbook's step 4 now points at the script. It also reads the artefact's
facts back afterwards, which is why this note's header can be trusted.

### Closed - the dictionary screens have tests

0.1.16: *"none of the new dictionary screens has a widget test... an editor that
can rewrite a published word deserves one before it is used in anger."*

`DictionaryAdminRepository` is now an interface (the Firebase one is
`FirebaseDictionaryAdminRepository`), which is what makes the screens pumpable at
all. **19 new screen tests**, all at 360x640 - the 720p phone in logical pixels:

- **`entry_editor_screen_test.dart`** (9) - a reason and a change is all it
  takes, and *only* the change is sent; an unchanged form refuses rather than
  sending an empty patch; a refusal from the server is said out loud; the bin is
  an admin key and asks in a box you can type in; a validator without the admin
  key is offered no bin at all.
- **`dictionary_screen_test.dart`** (6) - all three destinations stand up; the
  nav bar is on the bottom edge and is not a quarter of the screen; focusing the
  search box adds a strip and not a wall, above the rail rather than over it; a
  letter typed from the bar searches with it; the letter rail stays inside 360
  pixels.
- **`merge_screen_test.dart`** (4) - a reviewer who touches no row sends no
  choices, and the disposition defaults to retire so an old link still leads
  somewhere.

Plus the 10 from the fixes themselves (`kasem_key_bar_test.dart`,
`delete_reason_prompt_test.dart`). The height assertion was run against the
pre-fix code first and failed the way it should have - **600 pixels on a
600-pixel test surface**. A regression test nobody has watched fail is a
regression test nobody should trust.

One thing the editor tests found on the way: an entry stored
`partOfSpeech: 'noun'` is republished as `'Noun'` by any save, because the field
has always been free text and the editor sends the picker's label. Not a change
anybody made, so it is now pinned by a test that names it rather than left to be
rediscovered.

### Closed - the callables have emulator tests

**15 new tests** in `firebase/tests/dictionaryAdmin.test.mjs`, wired into
`npm run test:e2e`, covering exactly what the pure tests cannot: an ordinary
member cannot edit or delete; deleting outright is an admin key and so is
`disposition: 'delete'` on a merge; a headword can be corrected but never
emptied; a respelling moves the word to its new spelling group and takes the
next free number there; a merge fills blanks and never overwrites; a retried
merge is not a second merge and a retried delete does not fail; and the audit
row carries the reason.

Two behaviours the tests pinned rather than assumed: `editDictionaryEntry`
reports `englishTranslations` alongside `englishText` because it derives the
list from the summary line, and `mergedInto` is stored as
`{ collection, id }` rather than a bare id.

### Closed - the duplicate check has now run against the real archive

`npm run audit:duplicate-check -- --project project-kassena-7e026`, read-only.
It opens no transaction and writes nothing. What it found:

| | |
|---|---|
| rows in `dictionaryEntries` | **341** (338 published) |
| rows carrying **no** `headwordKey` | **235** - about 69% |
| rows whose stored key disagrees with its headword | 0 |

So the extra `kasemText` queries are not a fallback for an edge case: they are

real pairs waiting for the merge screen. Two of them - `nia pe yogo.` and
`we zoora.` - are sentences filed as headwords, and want a look before anybody
merges them.

### Still open - a physical device

- **The nav bar and the letter rail on real 720p hardware.** They are now
  asserted at 360x640 in a widget test, which catches the class of failure the

  gesture handle or a thumb. Only the key bar has been on a phone, and it was
  broken.
- **Edge-to-edge on a physical Android 15 device**, unchanged from 0.1.15.
- **The key-press preview itself has been on no phone.** It compiles
  (`:app:compileProductionReleaseKotlin`) and ships in this bundle; where it

  test cannot answer.

### Still open - not a code question

- **The digraph shortcuts on the system keyboard are still a draft** and want
