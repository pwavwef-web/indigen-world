0.1.16 shipped with a known gap written at the bottom of its own release note:
*"Nothing has been run on a device... the Kasem key bar's placement above the
keyboard, the nav bar, and the letter rail on a 720p screen are the three things
that most want a real phone."*

It went on a phone. The key bar's placement was the first thing to break, and it
broke completely: **tapping any Kasem field in the word editor covered the
entire form**. Not a misalignment - a wall of ceiling-to-floor lines with the
seven letters stranded halfway down it, painted over the app bar as well as the
form.

Every fix in this release is something a phone found in an afternoon and no test
in the suite could have seen, because the suite tested what the widgets *do* and
this was about how large they *are*. All three are now held by tests that
measure rather than tests that tap.

## Full change list

### Fixed - the Kasem letter bar covered the screen it was helping with

- **The bar is a strip along the bottom again.** `_Key` drew its glyph in a
  `Container` with an `alignment` and no height bound. A `Container` with an
  alignment grows to fill whatever height it is offered, and what a Scaffold
  offers its `bottomNavigationBar` is the height of the whole screen. So seven
  keys laid themselves out full-height; the Scaffold could not fit an
  800-pixel bar above the keyboard, applied its own `max(0, bottom - height)`
  rule and pinned the bar to `y=0`; and because the bottom bar is painted after
  the app bar, the result covered everything.

- **What that looked like** is worth recording, because it does not look like a
  layout bug. The key *borders* became seven vertical lines running the height
  of the display, the letters sat at the vertical centre of those lines, and the
  form was simply gone. Nothing in `lib/` that anybody had edited was involved.

- **The bar and each key now state their height** (`_kKeyHeight`, 40), in both
  places, with the reason written beside each. The community composer's bar had
  never had this problem because it has always been wrapped in a
  `SizedBox(height: 52)` - the same defence, arrived at by accident.

- **This was not only the editor.** The dictionary's Look up tab mounts the same
  bar above its nav rail, so focusing the search box did the same thing there.
  One fix, both screens.

### Fixed - the capital key was a lock, not a shift

- **One press meant capitals for the rest of the session.** The `AB` key
  latched. A key labelled "AB" invites exactly one exploratory press, and after
  it every letter the bar typed was a capital, with nothing but the row's own
  glyphs to say why. Reported from the field as "the Kasem keyboard sometimes
  types only capital letters", which is precisely what a silent latch looks like
  from the outside.

- **It is now a shift.** One capital, then it disarms itself - the way the shift
  on the keyboard below it behaves. Kasem takes a capital at the start of a
  sentence and on a name, which is one letter at a time; nobody types a headword
  in capitals, so a lock had no case to answer for.

- **It also disarms on leaving the Kasem boxes**, which closes the one remaining
  way an armed shift could outlive the letter it was meant for.

- **And it is legible now.** An up-arrow that fills with the accent colour while
  armed, instead of a two-letter label that showed the mode you were about to
  switch *to*.

### Fixed - deleting a word asked for a reason with nowhere to write it

- **The demand and the box are now in the same view.** The bin is in the app
  bar. The reason box was at the foot of a form several screens long, and the
  refusal - "Deleting needs a reason on the record. Write it first." - was
  written into an error line beside that box, below the fold, where the person
  who had just tapped the bin never saw it. What that produced in the field was
  somebody tapping every field on the screen looking for the place to write the
  reason they had been told was required.

- **`DeleteReasonPrompt`** is the confirmation and the reason box together.
  Delete stays disabled until there are ten characters in it - the same floor
  `deleteDictionaryEntry` enforces, checked on the phone so the button is
  visibly unavailable rather than failing on the server with the entry still on
  screen - and it counts down the characters still needed.

- **Anything already written in the form's own reason box is carried across**,
  so a reviewer who wrote their reason there first does not write it twice, and
  a delete that fails on the way out leaves the words where they can be edited
  and tried again.

- **A refused delete now speaks.** The failure was only ever written into that
  same off-screen error line; it is now also said in a toast, which floats above
  everything and is where the person is actually looking.

- **Worth naming:** with the bar covering the form, the whole editor was
  invisible the moment any Kasem field took focus. That is very likely half of
  why the reason box could not be found either. Two bugs, one symptom.

### New - the dictionary screens have widget tests now

0.1.16 listed "none of the new dictionary screens has a widget test" as a gap.
It is smaller today. Ten new tests, and they are the kind that would have caught
this:

- **`kasem_key_bar_test.dart`** (6) - the bar measures under 100 pixels and sits
  in the bottom half of the screen; there is no bar at all when no Kasem box has
  the cursor; every letter of the alphabet the keyboard lacks is offered; the
  capital key types one capital and then lets go; leaving the boxes drops an
  armed capital; a letter still lands at the caret rather than at the end.

- **`delete_reason_prompt_test.dart`** (4) - the button waits for a reason worth
  recording; the reason comes back trimmed; keeping the entry answers with
  nothing at all; a reason already on the form is carried across.

The height assertion was run against the old code before the fix went in, and
it failed the way it should have: the bar measured **600 pixels on a 600-pixel
test surface**. A regression test nobody has watched fail is a regression test
nobody should trust.

## Deployed alongside

- **Cloud Functions**, 2026-09-09 - **all 84 triggers**, in five explicit
  `--only` batches of 17, 84/84 successful. Not a new feature: the five
  dictionary callables 0.1.16 depends on were already live, and several shared
  modules had changed since the last partial deploy, so the whole backend was
  redeployed to end the bundle drift. Exports were 84 and live were 84 with an
  empty diff both ways *before* the deploy, which is what proved it could only
  be updates - no create and no possible deletion - and the same diff after.

- **Cold-start probes**, because a deploy log is not evidence a bundle boots.
  Each of `findDictionaryEntryMatches`, `previewDictionaryMerge`,
  `editDictionaryEntry`, `mergeDictionaryEntries` and `deleteDictionaryEntry`
  answered an unauthenticated call with `"Sign in is required."`, and
  `kawuriChat` with `"Ask a question first."` - their own messages, which a
  bundle with a broken import cannot produce.

- **Firestore rules, indexes and Storage rules**, 2026-09-09, re-released. The
  ruleset content was already current on the server, so the `mergedInto` read
  exception that makes the forwarding page work was live before this; it is
  confirmed rather than changed.

## Known gaps

- **Still nothing is committed.** Second bundle in a row from a dirty tree. See
  the note above; this is the oldest open item and the cheapest to close.

- **The build trap bit again, and will keep biting.**
  `android/app/src/main/java/io/flutter/plugins/GeneratedPluginRegistrant.java`
  is generated and untracked; a test run writes one that registers
  `integration_test`, and a release build that reuses it fails with `package
  dev.flutter.plugins.integration_test does not exist`. It was deleted before
  this build, and `flutter test` has since regenerated the stale version - so
  the next release build needs the same deletion first. A prebuild step that
  removes it would end this permanently.

- **The rest of 0.1.16's device gaps are still open.** The nav bar and the
  letter rail on a 720p screen have still not been confirmed on a phone; only
  the key bar has, and it was broken. The other two deserve the same look.

- **The editor's Save has the same shape as the delete did.** Its reason box and
  its error line sit directly above the Save button, so the demand and the
  answer are already in one view - but it is the same pattern, and if the form
  grows again it will develop the same problem.

- **No test covers the editor screen as a whole.** The key bar and the delete
  prompt are held; nothing pumps a frame of `EntryEditorScreen`, the merge
  screen, or the three dictionary destinations. Doing so needs a fake for
  `DictionaryAdminRepository`, which is currently a concrete class over
  `FirebaseFunctions` - an interface there is the unlock.

- **The merge and edit callables still have no emulator test**, unchanged from
  0.1.16. Their pure half is covered 25 ways; the transactions, role checks,
  renumbering query and audit writes are covered by reading.

- **The duplicate check has still never run against the real archive.**

- **There is still a stale AAB at a second path.**
  `apps/mobile/android/app/production/release/app-production-release.aab`, from
  2026-08-26, 76.1 MB, and it is not this release. Upload from
  `build/app/outputs/bundle/productionRelease/`.

- **Carried from 0.1.15 and 0.1.16 and still open:** the digraph shortcuts on
  the system keyboard are still a draft and want review with fluent speakers;
  the keyboard has no key-press popup preview; edge-to-edge has not been checked
  on a physical Android 15 device; and the TribeStudio dictionary desk has still
  not been visually confirmed in a browser.
