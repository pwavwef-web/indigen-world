The internet is becoming one of humanity’s most important places for learning, creating and belonging. Yet many indigenous languages and cultures remain difficult to find in search results, educational tools, digital services and emerging AI systems.

When a language has little digital presence, younger speakers have fewer places to use it. Stories can lose their context. Communities risk being represented by systems they did not help build.

We believe culture deserves more than a place in an archive.

Culture belongs in the future.

What is Indigen World?

Indigen World is a community-governed cultural technology ecosystem for language preservation, cultural learning, storytelling and creator enablement.

We are building connected digital spaces where communities can preserve knowledge, teach new generations, support cultural creators and decide how their heritage is represented online.

The ecosystem includes:

  • The Indigen World website, our public home for programmes, stories, partnerships and verified updates.
  • TribeStudio, a workspace in development for creators, contributors and cultural validators.
  • Indigen World Mobile, an offline-friendly experience in development for learners, families and diaspora communities.
  • Venacula, our flagship Kasem-language initiative and the first implementation of our language-cell model.

Each product has a different role, but they share one purpose: helping living cultures remain part of everyday digital life.

Starting with Kasem—deeply

Our first commitment is to Kasem, the language of the Kasena people of Northern Ghana.

Through Venacula, we are building the foundations for a community-validated Kasem dictionary, translation utility and structured language resource. The work is designed to respect dialect differences, preserve source information and keep contributor attribution and permissions attached to every record.

The planned process is straightforward but important:

  1. Community members contribute words, sentences, stories and cultural context.
  2. Teachers, elders and other qualified custodians review the material.
  3. Consent, attribution, dialect and cultural permissions remain connected to it.
  4. Approved knowledge can support learning, storytelling and responsible future technology.

These tools are still in development. We are beginning with one language so we can build the governance and validation model properly before considering expansion to other communities.

Built with communities, not around them

Technology alone cannot preserve a culture. The people who speak a language, carry its stories and understand its context must retain meaningful authority over how their knowledge is collected and used.

That is why community governance is part of our infrastructure—not paperwork added at the end.

For Indigen World, accuracy is not the only requirement. Validation does not automatically mean permission to publish. A dictionary entry, a personal story, a sacred expression and a voice recording may each require different treatment.

Our work is therefore guided by four commitments:

  • Clear consent
  • Respectful attribution
  • Qualified community validation
  • Cultural permissions that travel with the material

We are not building a digital museum. We are working toward living cultural infrastructure: technology communities can use to create, teach, learn and reconnect.

This is only the beginning

Indigen World is currently preparing its first Kasem pilot and continuing to develop the products that support it. We are learning carefully, building transparently and measuring progress without presenting ambitions as completed results.

We welcome Kasem speakers, teachers, elders, creators, schools, researchers, cultural organisations, technology partners, responsible funders and diaspora families who want to help.

Together, we can build a digital future in which indigenous cultures are not merely remembered. They are visible, useful, respected and alive.
