The Indigen World website is getting a new look. It moves back to a bold, deep blue, and the photographs at the top of every page now show real Kassena places and people: dancers and painted walls in Tiébélé, the crocodile pond at Paga and the basilica in Navrongo.

Coming soon to indigenworld.com. The redesign is built and tested, but it is not on the live website yet. This post will be updated when it is.

Back to blue

Earlier in October the website had moved to a cream and brown look with terracotta buttons. That palette did not match the rest of Indigen World: the Labs, the admin and contributor workspaces and the mobile app all use blue. The website now does too.

The new design uses one family of blues, from a deep night blue to a bright royal blue, with a single warm sun yellow, taken from the sun in the Indigen World logo, for the most important button on each dark area. Light sections use a cool, near-white background instead of cream.

Photographs from Kassena country

The banner at the top of each page used to show a single AI-generated drawing of fictional people, cropped three ways. It now shows five real photographs from the places where Kasem is spoken, on both sides of the Ghana–Burkina Faso border:

  • Dancers in Kassena country, Tiébélé, Burkina Faso, in red cowrie-shell caps beside a large drum.
  • The painted walls of a Kassena home in Tiébélé, with their black and white triangles and zigzags.
  • The crocodile pond at Paga, in Ghana's Upper East Region.
  • A painted Kassena compound in Tiébélé.
  • Our Lady of Seven Sorrows Basilica in Navrongo, Ghana.

Each slide names the place, the photographer and the licence while it is on screen, with links to the original. The banner moves to the next photograph every seven seconds and shows a small progress line under each name. You can pick a photograph, step through them with the arrows, or press Pause. If your device asks for reduced motion, nothing moves. Each page opens on a different photograph.

Where the photographs come from. All of them are openly licensed photographs on Wikimedia Commons. They document public places and events; they are not Indigen World community submissions. We will add community photographs as people share them with permission.

The banner also ends in a band of small chevrons, an original pattern inspired by the wall painting of Kassena compounds. The same band closes the page above the footer.

A homepage with more life

Every section of the homepage has been redesigned:

  • A word, front and centre. The reviewed Kasem word from the dictionary now sits in a large dark-blue card, with its meaning, its recording and its source. The everyday expression and the grammar guide sit beside it.
  • Ghana on the map. The "Rooted in a place" section is now a night-blue band where Ghana is the lit country.
  • Real places in every story. The three "Build with us" cards now carry photographs from Navrongo and Tiébélé instead of the same drawing repeated three times.
  • A royal-blue close. The page ends with a bright blue section about the people behind the knowledge, before the footer.

Every page follows

The blue design and the photograph banner reach every public page: About, Ecosystem, Project Kassena, the dictionary, Learn, Contribute, Impact and governance, Get involved, Contact, Privacy and Terms, as well as the standalone grammar and spelling guides. Headings use a stronger typeface, and Kasem words keep a typeface that shows every Kasem letter and tone mark correctly.

What has not changed

Nothing about how the website works has changed. The dictionary search, saved words, the contribution form, the newsletter sign-up and every link work exactly as before, and no account is needed to browse. The Labs and the progress page keep their own designs.

Who it is for

Everyone who visits indigenworld.com: Kasem speakers, learners, teachers, researchers and partners. The website now looks like the rest of Indigen World, and the first thing you see is the culture it serves.

When the redesign is live, open indigenworld.com and let the photographs play, or press Pause and look around. If something looks wrong on your device, tell us through the contact page.
