A Kasem word, story or proverb deserves more than a translation box. Its source, the situation in which it is used, its regional forms and the people who check it all matter. The new Knowledge workspace brings those details into one contribution and keeps them together through human review.

Choose the kind of knowledge you are preserving

The workspace offers ten areas: words and meanings, grammar, expressions, sentences, proverbs, literature, dialogue, pronunciation, cultural knowledge, and questions and answers. Each has its own prompts. A proverb asks about literal meaning and interpretation; a dialogue asks about speaker turns and relationships; a recording asks for its transcript, speaker reference and recording conditions.

Contributors can preserve original Kasem separately from English and optional French translations. They can explain context, identify the source and region, describe variants, and reference related records. A missing equivalent or unresolved analysis can be recorded honestly. The form does not need an invented answer.

Build the record a little at a time

In the app, open Contribute → Knowledge workspace. On the website, follow the Knowledge workspace link on the Contribute page. Dictionary entries also offer a link to contribute context or a pronunciation; the new record carries the exact dictionary entry reference.

Sign in, choose an area, add the material and its source, then save a draft or send it for review. Drafts can be incomplete. Required information is checked before submission, and each record receives a permanent ID. Multiple recordings can distinguish isolated pronunciation from speech in context, with a separate transcript and speaker reference for each.

Review the evidence and preserve disagreement

Authorized reviewers use TribeStudio to assess the submitted record. They confirm competence in its language and dialect, and in its cultural context where needed. A contributor cannot independently authenticate their own work.

Gold requires two independent approvals of the same revision. A request for changes or a disagreement prevents Gold. Review explanations remain part of the record's history. When a contributor edits the material, that revision needs fresh review; earlier approval does not silently transfer to new wording.

Completeness checks help people see what is missing. They do not establish whether a statement is true. Human review is a separate step.

Permission stays with the contribution

Contributors make separate choices for review, future public display, AI-provider retrieval, model training, evaluation and recording storage. These choices begin unchecked. The source terms and cultural access level are recorded too. Restricted and sensitive material cannot grant public display, AI retrieval or model training through this workspace.

The workspace is for collection and review. A Gold label does not automatically publish a dictionary entry, add material to Kawuri's answers or train a model. Those downstream steps require a separate integration and the appropriate permissions. Existing dictionary and sentence-evidence workflows continue to serve their current purposes.

Who this helps

Speakers can contribute the details they know without completing everything in one sitting. Teachers and reviewers can check a specific version with its evidence. Cultural custodians can preserve context and legitimate variation. The wider community benefits from records that explain where knowledge came from and what is still uncertain.

The first collection catalogue is for Kasem. Reviewers need assigned access, and saved records need an internet connection. Live availability will be confirmed after the backend, websites and app release are verified.
