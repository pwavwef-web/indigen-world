Beyond the Reef is a song about leaving the safety of the shore to carry languages, stories and people into the future. It now has a home on indigenworld.com as an interactive music film: press play, and the words rise with the song while a small canoe sails from a quiet beach, past the reef, through a storm and on toward the horizon.

LiveAt indigenworld.com/beyond-the-reef, on phones and computers, in an up-to-date web browser. It is free and needs no account. Turn your sound on.

What is new at a glance

  • A music film you can play, pause and explore: the full song, five minutes and sixteen seconds, in a full-screen page.
  • Lyrics that move with the song: each line rises into the middle of the screen as it is sung, and the line being sung is the brightest.
  • A journey in eleven chapters: from the shore at blue hour to a sunrise over open water, with the chapter name shown as you go.
  • Player controls: play and pause, restart, a seek bar with chapter marks, volume, mute and full screen, plus keyboard shortcuts.
  • Made for every screen: large, readable lyrics and thumb-sized controls on a phone.
  • Gentle on data and motion: only the scenes near where you are load, and the film calms itself if your device asks for reduced motion.

How to watch

  1. Open indigenworld.com/beyond-the-reef. The song does not start until you choose to begin.
  2. Press Begin the Journey. The opening screen fades away and the song starts.
  3. Watch, or take control: drag the seek bar to jump to any moment. Small marks on the bar show where each chapter begins.
  4. When the song ends, choose Replay to sail again, or Return to site.
TipOn a computer, press F for full screen, Space to pause, and ← or → to move five seconds. M mutes, R restarts, and the number keys jump through the song: 5 takes you halfway.

Lyrics that move with the song

The lyrics roll upward like the credits of a film, but they know where the singer is. The line being sung sits just below the middle of the screen and glows; the next line glides into its place a moment before it is sung, and the lines already sung drift upward and fade.

Everything on the page follows the song itself. Pause, and the words and pictures pause with it. Jump back to the first chorus or forward to the storm, and the right line and the right scene are there straight away. There are no separate timers that could slowly drift out of step.

Every word is exactly as written. The timings for all 120 lines were measured from the recording word by word, then checked against short excerpts of the song.

A journey in eleven chapters

The pictures follow the song's story, and the light moves with it, from blue hour to starlight, sunrise, midday, dusk, a stormy night, moonlight and a misty dawn.

  1. The Shore: a canoe waits on a quiet beach under the last stars.
  2. Maps and Screens: the lights of a restless city across the water.
  3. What I'm Carrying: a lantern held close in the bottom of the boat.
  4. Beyond the Reef: the canoe crosses the reef into deep blue water at sunrise.
  5. Beneath the Surface: the words inside our mothers, held beneath the waves.
  6. One Shore Done Right: one island, one fire, and a second light following behind.
  7. Nightfall: sparks of light rise from the water like voices filling the air.
  8. The Storm: the song's quietest, hardest questions, asked in the middle of a storm.
  9. Discovery: mist lifts from an island at dawn.
  10. Carry the People: a long line of lantern-lit canoes follows across the sea.
  11. The Horizon: the sun is up, and the traveller knows what they are sailing for.

On a phone

On a phone the lyrics stay the star. They are set large and wrap neatly on narrow screens, the pictures are framed so the canoe stays in view, and the controls sit at the bottom within reach of your thumb. The controls stay visible on touch screens; on a computer they fade away while the film plays and come back when you move the mouse or press a key.

Where your phone supports it, its lock screen and headphone buttons can play and pause the song. Your volume setting is remembered for next time.

How the film was made

The pictures and film clips were made with generative AI, and the page says so. The still images were drawn with Google's Gemini image model, all from one reference picture of the same small wooden canoe, so the boat, its painted band, its sail and its brass lantern look the same in every scene. Eight short, silent film clips were then made with Google's Veo, each starting from its own picture: waves around the canoe on the shore, stars wheeling over the lagoon, a flight above the reef, sunlight under the water, a golden-hour crossing, the storm, the mist, and the fleet.

The only person in the film is a lone traveller, always seen from behind or far away. No real people or places are shown, and there is no writing in any of the pictures.

NoteAll of the pictures and clips were made in advance and are part of the page. Watching the film does not send anything to an AI service, and it does not need an account.

Light on data, and on motion

The page only loads the scenes around the moment you are watching, so starting the film does not download the whole thing. Watching all five minutes uses roughly 20 to 25 MB, about the same as a few minutes of low-resolution video.

If your device is set to reduce motion, the film shows each scene as a still picture with a soft fade between them, and the lyrics step from line to line instead of gliding. Screen readers are offered each line as it is sung, and the complete lyrics are also on the page as plain text.
