Written letters and sound symbols

The book gives seven written vowels a e ɛ i o ɔ u. Its notes say c and h occur together as ch, and n and y can form ny. ŋ can begin a word. Phonetic vowel symbols describe pronunciation rather than extra ordinary written letters. Tone distinctions in this grammar

Tone is part of a word's pronunciation. This grammar describes low mid high and rising tones but usually leaves tone unwritten. It explicitly marks á plural you, ná if, bá will not and wó will to distinguish them from other words. This is a source-specific convention, not a general accent rule.

Five noun classes

Nouns are grouped into classes A B C D E by their singular and plural pronouns and articles. Stems combine with suffixes; the book distinguishes consonant and vowel stems and describes class-specific changes. Class is not determined only by a noun's meaning.

Noun class A

Class A uses o and ba, and articles wom and bam. Singular endings include o or u; plural endings include a and na. Many members denote people, but some people nouns belong to other classes.

HOW TO USE THIS BOOK

This book has been written to help you to understand the grammar of Kasem. If Kasem is not your own language and you want to learn it, then it will help you. You need to ask a Kasena person to speak to you so that you can hear the words as well as see them written down.

If Kasem is your own language and you want to know more about it, or if you are a linguist (someone who is interested in languages), this book will explain the basic grammar. It does not use special, technical language, so that as many people as possible will be able to use it.

This book may be used for teaching kasem at SHS

Because this book has been written for people with different needs, some of it may not be interesting to you. The second half of chapter 1 contains notes which may be of interest mostly to linguists. Chapter 2 is written for those who want to start learning Kasem. If Kasem is your own language, you may like to leave this part out.

Chapters 3 and 8 cover subjects which get more difficult as you go along. You should read them in the right order. Chapter 9 may be most useful as a reference guide.
