# Indigen World implementation evidence audit

Audit date: 15 October 2026. Scope: repository documentation, product routes, backend entry points and selected implementation/security/test files. This is a bounded source audit, **not a fresh production acceptance test**, a census of live content or an audit of private records. No secrets, private correspondence, production exports, customer records or provider account settings were read; no deployment, import, external publication or message was performed. The dashboard should retain that boundary.

## Evidence vocabulary

- **Implemented:** executable product/backend code exists, with the cited behavior visible in the source. This does not mean the latest checkout passed tests or is deployed.
- **Test coverage exists:** the named tests exercise an invariant. Their existence is different from a passing run in this audit.
- **Documented verification / release:** a dated repository record reports a result. Preserve its date, scope and limitations; it is not a fresh independent live observation.
- **Gated / operational decision:** a working path needs grants, configuration, billing, content rights, reviewer authority or owner action before availability.
- **Planned:** explicit design direction without demonstrated completion. Do not turn it into a live feature.
- **Outcome unknown:** actual learning, retention, cultural legitimacy, moderation effectiveness, contributor satisfaction and financial sustainability require measurement outside source inspection.

No overall “platform completion percentage” is defensible from this audit: the vision has no agreed weighted acceptance criteria, and code, deployment, qualified content, operational coverage and user outcomes are different denominators.

## The intended complete platform

The north star is an umbrella cultural-technology ecosystem for language preservation, cultural learning, storytelling, creator enablement and community-governed digital heritage—not only a Kasem dictionary. The public website, TribeStudio, everyday mobile app and internal administration support different audiences. Project Kassena is the first language cell; its Kasem-specific linguistic and cultural authority should survive later expansion. Sources: `README.md:3–11`; `docs/product/product-boundaries.md:5–19,21–61,77–89`.

The intended operating loop is: discover and learn → use culture and language in daily community life → contribute with context and permissions → independent review and correction → publish to appropriate products → create permission-specific research/retrieval/training releases → measure results and reinvest in contributors. Publication, retrieval, training and evaluation are distinct uses. Restricted/sacred material must remain outside ordinary public release. Sources: `docs/product/product-boundaries.md:54–71,79–87`; `docs/governance/data-safety.md:18–40,46–48`; `services/functions/src/knowledge-policy.ts:77–99`; `services/functions/src/knowledge-release.ts:44–75,149–156`.

Three enduring design requirements are community governance, low-bandwidth/offline use and provider-independent AI behind trusted services. Firebase is authoritative; DigitalOcean is a planned bounded extension, not a replacement backend already running in the tree. Sources: `README.md:52–59,61–63`; `docs/architecture/repository-structure.md:33–57`; `docs/product/product-boundaries.md:63–75`.

## Material gaps and contradictions

1. **Governed-export UI overclaims enforcement (high priority).** `apps/admin/src/console/ExportManager.tsx:35–53` fetches application records, the review queue, campaigns or website interests and shallow-copies records. The fetches return full document data, and the queue includes submitted/resubmitted/under-review rows, not only validated work (`apps/admin/src/creators/data.ts:139–145,277–283`; `apps/admin/src/interests/data.ts:105–120`). Website intake stores contact details in nested payloads (`services/functions/src/public-forms.ts:130–134,163–167`); removing two top-level fields is therefore not personal-data minimization. The export's default restriction removal deletes only `sacredMetadata` and `privateNotes`. The restricted checkbox is local state with no clearance-record validation in this component (`apps/admin/src/console/ExportManager.tsx:119–124`); the title/body promise “permission-safe” packages and recorded Elder Council clearance (`:99–102`). The export is serialized and downloaded in the client (`:55–88`). This does **not** establish use-specific rights screening, approved-only submissions, a clearance record, an allowlisted public projection or an export audit event. Admin read authorization is not the same as authorization to share the whole raw document with a research partner. Route it through a trusted, audited, typed export operation with explicit permission checks; add denial tests. Keep this separate from the much stronger `services/functions/src/knowledge-release.ts:149–156` export path. Do not export or inspect real records to demonstrate the issue.

2. **“All reviewed culture” would contradict implemented open publishing.** The ordinary open route intentionally has no reviewer, and moderation is reactive (`services/functions/src/open-publishing.ts:11–28,66–99`). Preserve public publication-route/authentication labels and establish clear report/takedown staffing and service goals. The shared projection explicitly marks authentication unspecified outside collection review (`services/functions/src/publication.ts:292–309`).

3. **Product boundary drift needs a decision.** The approved document says Admin owns oversight but individual reviews live in Studio, and mobile does not replace professional validation (`docs/product/product-boundaries.md:29,33,42,61`). Actual Admin navigation has a complete Review Desk with publication decisions (`apps/admin/src/routes.ts:137–145`), while mobile has validation screens/tests (`apps/mobile/lib/features/validate/validate_screen.dart`; `apps/mobile/test/features/validate/validate_test.dart`). The practical distinction may be acceptable, but the authoritative boundary document must match the operating design. Root README also still places administration inside TribeStudio (`README.md:8`).

4. **Cross-app imports contradict stated dependency rules.** Architecture prohibits one app depending directly on another (`docs/architecture/repository-structure.md:53`). Admin imports the Studio BrandMark (`apps/admin/src/App.tsx:4`). This is a concrete shared-package extraction candidate; describe the current console as consistent in appearance, not fully independent in implementation.

5. **Local/production isolation is easy to misunderstand.** Mobile README says emulator traffic is the default (`apps/mobile/README.md:27`) but later says every flavour talks to the real shared project unless opted into emulators (`:99,122–129`). Code confirms explicit opt-in only (`apps/mobile/lib/core/app_config.dart:24–46`), and Firebase initialization guards emulator connection failures while retaining the real project (`apps/mobile/lib/core/firebase_bootstrap.dart:63–67`). Correct the stale sentence, make environment identity unmistakable in developer workflows and verify test startup cannot fall through to live writes. Different Firebase app registrations are not different backend environments.

6. **Feature-default comments and defaults disagree.** Firebase bootstrap says the Play review prompt “ships dark” but the actual default is `rating_prompt_enabled: true` (`apps/mobile/lib/core/firebase_bootstrap.dart:130–134`). This is a source-level contradiction. Confirm intended rollout and live Remote Config before making any product claim. Other feature defaults are false (`:124–129`); their effective use and server gates must be checked separately rather than interpreting every flag as a current availability statement.

7. **App Check integration is different from enforcement.** Knowledge and Labs callable enforcement depends on `ENFORCE_APP_CHECK === 'true'` (`services/functions/src/knowledge-workspace.ts:15`; `services/functions/src/knowledge-release.ts:9`; `services/functions/src/labs.ts:43`), while mobile activates App Check best-effort (`apps/mobile/lib/core/firebase_bootstrap.dart:109,149–158`). This audit did not inspect deployed environment flags. Do not label App Check enforced ecosystem-wide based on SDK presence. Authenticated deployed transport acceptance remains a recorded gap (`docs/product/releases/0.1.32+41.md:52`).

8. **Trusted/audited mutation is not universal just because it is a principle.** Backend README requires every privileged mutation to authenticate, authorize, validate and audit (`services/functions/README.md:25–28`), but rules allow admin client writes for configuration/language/community metadata (`firebase/firestore.rules:322–338,666–668`). This is not automatically a vulnerability; it is a concrete review question: document which direct-admin paths create audits, or move sensitive transitions behind trusted operations. The generic export path already demonstrates why a blanket guarantee is unsafe.

9. **Model/training readiness has human and data gates.** Source-backed retrieval, schemas, release tools and synthetic tests do not establish a trained Kasem model, qualified linguistic reviewers, approved cultural protocols, sufficient consent or a released training set (`docs/product/product-boundaries.md:71`; `docs/product/kawuri-corpus-implementation.md:3–5,60–72`; `docs/product/kasem-evidence-operations.md:3,55–67`). Publish separate readiness for text retrieval, training, evaluation, ASR/TTS and translation. Held-out/source-family checks are important implemented safeguards (`services/functions/src/knowledge-policy.ts:87–94`; `services/functions/src/knowledge-release.ts:64–73`).

10. **Research/retrieval completeness is bounded.** Candidate retrieval has documented selector/page limits and truncation, while legacy sentence-family computation can withhold the source past 4,000 rows; candidate readiness/read flags were not enabled in the cited release (`docs/operations/ten-improvements-verification.md:103–118`; `docs/product/releases/0.1.32+41.md:52`). Verify a current dry run, independent rights checks, backfill coverage, regression results and readiness before a rollout claim.

11. **A release asset is not a distribution outcome.** The 9 Oct document records a signed AAB and GitHub publication, but explicitly says Play submission/rollout and physical-device audio/Google sign-in/reviewer App Check remain unverified (`docs/product/releases/0.1.32+41.md:39–52`). Treat the public mobile availability label as a separate owner-confirmed fact, not something inferred from build metadata.

12. **Documentation freshness is uneven.** Backend README still asks for package/build/tests “when scaffolded” even though many implemented modules and test scripts exist (`services/functions/README.md:30–36`; `services/functions/src/index.ts:5–225`; `package.json:23–44`). Public shared-foundation status says “research” while root README says implemented/building infrastructure (`apps/website/src/content/ecosystem.ts:62–70`; `README.md:61–63`). Update these states through one evidence register rather than broad optimistic or pessimistic labels.

13. **Legal/community licence readiness is separate from technology.** No repository-wide open-source licence is approved, and software/cultural data/brand/third-party materials need separate treatment (`docs/governance/licensing-status.md:3–18`). Website legal summaries are pending legal approval (`apps/website/README.md:57`). No legal-readiness claim follows from a privacy/terms route.

14. **Provider/account activation remains a real release gate.** WhatsApp support exports are only loaded with `ENABLE_SUPPORT_WHATSAPP=true`, with a source comment waiting for owner Meta onboarding (`services/functions/src/index.ts:208–218`). Do not present a connected WhatsApp support channel merely because its module exists. Apply the same separate proof discipline to SMS, Play Billing, AI video, ads and payout providers.

15. **Learner/community outcomes are unmeasured in this audit.** Coarse dictionary analytics explicitly do not establish satisfaction/retention (`apps/website/README.md:77–79`). There is no inspected representative-user research, baseline retention, validated learning-gain study, moderation service-level evidence, settled reward report or sustainability model here. Record these as unknown/measurement needed, never zero and never positive by implication.

## Suggested honest roadmap for the dashboard

1. **Trust defects first:** replace the generic Admin “governed export” with a trusted allowlisted policy/audit path; reconcile publication/authentication labels; correct environment/default contradictions. Acceptance evidence: negative authorization/clearance tests, synthetic export fixtures and matched public wording.
2. **Prove complete production journeys:** a signed-in contributor draft → review → correction → publication → points assessment, a reviewer race/stale decision, creator render/download, notification permission/delivery, Play purchase/reconciliation, offline playback and sign-out/account-switch flows. Use synthetic accounts/content and record device, transport, environment and version.
3. **Agree cultural/research authority:** named accountable reviewer roles, scope/quorum, approved consent/withdrawal protocol, per-use licences and held-out dataset decisions. Sources for unresolved owner decisions: `docs/product/kawuri-corpus-implementation.md:60–72`; `docs/product/kasem-evidence-operations.md:55–67`.
4. **Verify distribution and operations:** current deployed commit/hosting/function/index/flag inventory, Play track/version approval, provider readiness, moderation/support coverage, budgets and incident procedures. Dated release evidence should be marked stale until refreshed.
5. **Measure whether it works for people:** agree baselines/definitions for activation, repeat learning, successful contribution/review time, grounded-answer reliability, content-rights coverage, report response, settled rewards, offline success and cost per useful outcome. Always show denominator/sample/date and consent boundaries.
6. **Grow the complete ecosystem deliberately:** language/community expansion, school/family/teacher use, creators and cultural custodians, music/literature/storytelling, community daily use, research partnerships and future voice/translation. Expand after the Kasem operating loop has acceptance evidence; generic schemas alone do not make a second language cell ready. Sources: `README.md:3–11,54–59`; `docs/product/product-boundaries.md:9,23,52,77–89`.

Recommended dashboard filters: product, audience, capability, evidence level, environment, source date, operational owner and next acceptance proof. Recommended status presentation: show **implementation**, **verification**, **deployment** and **outcome** as separate fields. Unknown should be a first-class value.
