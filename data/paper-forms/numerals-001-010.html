Kasem number sheets — counting one to ten

Batch 1 · ten things to count · one page each · keep this page beside you while you write

What these sheets are for

A number in Kasem is not always said the same way. Which way it is said depends on what is being counted — so the question “how do you say two?” has no single answer, and nobody has ever written down the whole of it. Each sheet gives you one ordinary thing and asks you to count it from one to ten. Put side by side afterwards, the twelve sheets show something no single sheet can.

How to fill in a sheet

  1. Read the word at the top of the sheet. Write it in Kasem on the first line, large.
  2. Answer the short questions about the word itself.
  3. Then count. Say the whole phrase out loud first, then write down exactly what you said. Do not work it out as grammar — say it.
  4. Write the number and the word together, every line, even when it feels repetitive. Writing only the part that changed is what would make these sheets worthless.
  5. If a number can be said in more than one way for that word, write both, and say underneath when each one is used.
  6. Any line you are not sure about, leave it empty. A guess becomes a dictionary entry that teaches somebody the wrong thing.
  7. Sign the bottom and write where you are from. If more than one person worked on a sheet, write both names.

The last two sheets are yours

N11 and N12 have no word printed on them. Choose your own — and if you know a word that is counted differently from the ten here, that is the one worth writing. A word somebody noticed is worth more than a word off a list.

If you run out of room

Write on the back, and put the sheet code (C1, N01, N02…) at the top of what you write there. Keep the sheets in order and send them back together — including any you could not answer. A sheet nobody could fill in is something worth knowing too.

What a filled-in line looks like in English — yours will be in Kasem

dog
Written out in full every time, the number and the word together — never just the part that changed.
1one dog
2two dogs
3three dogs
4four dogs
Why in full. In English the number never changes — two is two whatever is being counted, and only the word for the thing changes. That is the part these sheets cannot assume. If the number itself sounds different when you count different things, the only way anybody will ever know is if you have written the number out each time.
If you are unsure of a line, leave it empty. An empty line is honest.
